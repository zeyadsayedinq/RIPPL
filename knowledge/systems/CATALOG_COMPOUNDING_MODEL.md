# Catalog Compounding Model

## Theory

Each released track is a permanent piece of search surface. Discovery probability
scales with the number of *distinct, well-titled, well-tagged* entry points, not
with the quality of any one entry point.

Simplified: `monthly_passive_discovery ≈ Σ(track_i × search_surface_i × decay_i)`

Two levers you control: **number of tracks** and **search surface per track**
(title, alt titles, tags, description, playlist placement, video version).
Decay is mostly outside your control — which is exactly why volume matters.

## Rules

1. **Every track ships with a video version.** Two surfaces for one master.
2. **Title for search, not for poetry.** The searchable phrase goes first.
3. **Never delete catalog to "clean up".** Archive from the front page, keep the URL.
4. **Re-pitch old catalog quarterly.** Editorial teams change; your back catalog
   is new to them.
5. **Bundle after 10.** Compilations, "best of", seasonal packs — new surface,
   zero new production cost.

## Cadence targets

| Stage | Releases / quarter | Video versions | Playlist pitches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building | 6–9 | All | 12 |
| Established | 3–4 + 1 project | All | 20 (incl. back catalog) |
| Scaling | 2 projects + catalog work | All | 30+ |

## Measurement

Track in `/dashboard`: share of monthly streams coming from tracks older than
90 days. Healthy independent catalog: **>40%**. Below 25% means you have a
release business, not a catalog business — every quiet month costs you.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every track has a video version live
- [ ] Titles contain the actual search phrase
- [ ] Quarterly back-catalog re-pitch is scheduled in `/calendar`
- [ ] Catalog share of streams reported monthly
