# Objection Handling

Objections are information about a gap in the offer, not resistance to be overcome.
Handle the gap; don't argue.

| Objection | What it usually means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive" | Value isn't legible | Restate the outcome and what's included; offer a smaller scope, never a lower price for the same scope |
| "Let me think about it" | Missing a decision criterion | "What would you need to know to decide?" |
| "Send me a proposal" | Polite exit, or a real process | Ask what the proposal needs to answer; if they can't say, it's an exit |
| "We're working with someone else" | Not a rejection, a timing issue | Ask when the current arrangement is reviewed; diary it in `/roster` |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | Testing, or genuinely budget-capped | Reduce deliverables, extend the timeline, or take a smaller first job |
| "We don't have budget this quarter" | Real, and useful | Get the exact date budget resets; log it |
| "Just send the files and we'll pay after" | Risk transfer to you | 50% up front, standard, no exceptions |

## Rules

1. **Never discount without removing scope.** A discount without scope change
   teaches the client your price was fiction.
2. **Ask one question before answering.** You cannot address a gap you haven't
   identified.
3. **Write the objection into `/roster`.** The same three objections will repeat;
   fix the offer, not the pitch.
4. **Deposit is not negotiable.** It is the single highest-leverage term in a
   service business.

## Checklist

- [ ] Objection logged against the deal
- [ ] Price never moved without scope moving
- [ ] 50% deposit terms stated in writing before work starts
